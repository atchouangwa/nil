# NIL Deal Accelerator VSL Scripts

These scripts are written for Brian "BB" Knight to deliver in first person. They follow the VSL That Converts framework and the Confirmation Page That Converts framework supplied for this funnel.

The core objective is not to use bigger words. It is to move the athlete through the beliefs required to make a rational purchase decision:

1. NIL opportunity can be relevant to an athlete like me.
2. Marketability can be deliberately built.
3. Random posting, random outreach, and waiting for inbound opportunities is not a system.
4. I can build a brand and pursue opportunities without becoming fake or desperate.
5. Personalized implementation is different from consuming more free information.
6. Building this now can be worth the investment because my college-athlete window is temporary, while the skills, brand, relationships, and systems can last beyond it.

## Production Direction

- Presenter: Brian "BB" Knight
- Primary format: talking head, with simple on-screen text and occasional screen examples
- Delivery: conversational, certain, athlete-to-athlete, not corporate
- Environment: clean training facility, basketball gym, athlete apartment, or simple studio that still feels authentic to BB
- Wardrobe: elevated athlete casual. Team gear, clean neutral athletic wear, or a simple NIL Playbook shirt. Do not dress like a finance guru.
- Editing: tight cuts, subtle captions, occasional supporting screenshots, no loud motion graphics
- Claims: conservative, specific, and attributable
- No guarantees of sponsorships, income, follower growth, brand responses, or timelines
- When the script references BB contracting over $12,000 from social media, keep the word "contracted" exactly. Do not rewrite it as earned, collected, or made.
- Wistia: use a real video thumbnail for the primary VSL. On the confirmation page, use static image thumbnails for breakout videos so multiple animated thumbnails do not compete with one another.

## Word-for-word script

### Hook

Hey, I am Brian "BB" Knight.

If you are a college athlete who knows NIL matters, but you still do not have a clear system for turning who you are, the audience you have, and the story you are living into real opportunities, this is for you.

You do not need to be the biggest name on your roster. You do not need to have hundreds of thousands of followers. And you do not need to turn yourself into a fake influencer.

### Credibility

I am saying that as someone who is still living this.

I play Division I basketball. I have had to deal with the same practices, lifts, travel, schoolwork, injuries, transfers, and limited time that every college athlete deals with.

Years ago, I happened to capture a video of myself shattering a backboard. That video went viral, and it changed how I thought about attention.

It showed me that one moment could reach people far beyond the gym.

So I started taking content more seriously. I kept documenting my life, learning how to edit, learning what people responded to, building an audience, and figuring out how to turn attention into something more useful than views.

Last month, I contracted over $12,000 from social media while still playing Division I basketball.

That is my experience. I am not telling you that because I think you are automatically going to do the same thing. You are not guaranteed a deal, a dollar amount, a follower count, or a timeline.

I am telling you because I have had to solve the exact problem this program is built around: how do you take being an athlete seriously while also building something off the court?

### Buying motive 1: More control over opportunity

The first reason athletes come to us is simple. They want more control.

A lot of athletes approach NIL like this: post when you remember, hope the post does well, join a marketplace, wait for a brand to message you, maybe send a few DMs, then hope something happens.

That is not a strategy. That is random NIL.

And random NIL creates random results.

If a brand lands on your page today, can they quickly understand who you are beyond your sport?

Can they see what you care about?

Can they understand who follows you and why?

Can they picture what a partnership with you could look like?

Can they tell that you will be professional when money, deliverables, deadlines, usage rights, and communication are involved?

If the answer is unclear, the issue may not be that you are not talented enough or popular enough.

The issue may be that your marketability is not packaged clearly enough for somebody else to understand.

### Buying motive 2: Build something that lasts beyond the jersey

The second reason is bigger than one deal.

Your college-athlete career has a clock on it.

That is not meant to be dramatic. It is just true.

At some point, the last game happens.

The question is what you leave with.

You can leave with highlights, stats, pictures, and memories. Those matter.

But you can also leave with a recognizable personal brand, content skills, an audience, professional relationships, experience working with companies, and a system for creating opportunities outside of your sport.

That is why I think your jersey is a vehicle, not the final destination.

Basketball has given me attention, relationships, discipline, and a story. I want to use those things to build something bigger than the game itself.

And I want other athletes to understand how to do the same thing in a way that fits who they actually are.

### Buying motive 3: Do it without letting the sport suffer

The third reason is time.

The hardest part for me has never been understanding that content matters.

The hardest part has been finding time to film, edit, post, respond, learn, and handle opportunities while basketball still demands most of my attention.

That matters because I do not believe your brand should require you to stop being an athlete.

The system has to work around practice, class, travel, games, recovery, and your actual life.

If your NIL plan only works when you have unlimited free time, it is not a real athlete NIL plan.

### The problem mechanism: The Marketability Gap

This is where I want to introduce the idea behind the program.

We call it the Marketability Gap.

The Marketability Gap is the distance between having raw potential and having a brand that a company can clearly understand, evaluate, and work with.

You can have athletic credibility and still have unclear positioning.

You can have followers and still have no idea what your content should consistently communicate.

You can post all the time and still have no media kit, no clear partner categories, no outreach process, and no system for tracking opportunities.

You can get a deal and still not know how to think about pricing, deliverables, communication, renewal, or whether the deal even fits your brand.

Those are not five separate problems.

They are symptoms of not having an operating system.

### The solution mechanism: 5-Play NIL Marketability System

That is what we built NIL Deal Accelerator around.

There are five plays.

Play one is Position.

We clarify your story, values, interests, audience, differentiators, and the types of brands that make sense for you.

Play two is Publish.

We build a content system you can actually execute around being a college athlete.

Play three is Package.

We create the professional assets that help brands understand you, including your media kit, brand-facing bio, partnership concepts, and offer structure.

Play four is Pursue.

We build your target-brand criteria, outreach messaging, follow-up process, and opportunity pipeline so you are not completely dependent on getting discovered.

Play five is Professionalize.

We help you prepare for brand conversations, rates, deliverables, negotiation, compliance handoff, performance, and renewal.

That is the difference between random NIL and a professional NIL system.

### The offer

NIL Deal Accelerator is a 12-week high-touch implementation program.

This is not just a library of videos.

We work with you on your NIL marketability audit, personal positioning, content pillars, 90-day content roadmap, profile optimization, professional media kit, partner-category strategy, target-brand pipeline, outreach messaging, follow-up process, rate and package guidance, weekly coaching, content review, brand-call preparation, negotiation preparation, deal-evaluation frameworks, deliverable management, renewal strategy, and your next-quarter growth plan.

### Objection 1: I do not have enough followers

You might be thinking, "I do not have enough followers yet."

Follower count can matter. I am not going to pretend it does not.

But follower count alone does not tell a brand your story, audience fit, professionalism, content quality, local relevance, personality, reliability, or whether you can create something useful for them.

The goal is not to convince you that followers never matter.

The goal is to stop treating follower count like the only variable you can improve.

### Objection 2: I do not have time

You might also be thinking, "I barely have time now."

I get that.

I built my own platform while playing college basketball. I know what it feels like when the sport still has to come first.

That is exactly why we focus on systems, repeatable content formats, clear priorities, and a plan you can execute instead of asking you to live on your phone all day.

### Objection 3: I do not want to become an influencer

You do not have to.

I do not think the goal is to turn every athlete into the same kind of creator.

Your brand should be built around what is already true about you: your story, your interests, your values, your personality, your community, your goals, and your sport.

### Objection 4: I can learn this for free

You absolutely can learn a lot of NIL for free.

You can watch videos. You can ask ChatGPT. You can search TikTok. You can read articles. You can ask teammates.

Information is not the scarce part anymore.

The question is whether you want to spend the next six or twelve months figuring out which advice applies to you, building every asset from scratch, guessing at your positioning, testing outreach by yourself, and trying to hold yourself accountable while your season keeps moving.

The Accelerator is not priced like information because it is not an information product.

It is personalized strategy, implementation, feedback, accountability, professional assets, and a system built around you.

### Objection 5: The investment

And if $4,997 is a serious financial decision for you, that is normal.

Do not pretend it is not a lot of money.

For some athletes, that means talking with a parent or guardian. For some, it means comparing this against other things they have been saving for. For others, the answer may simply be that now is not the right time.

The call is there to help you understand what we would build, whether the fit is real, and whether the investment makes sense for your situation.

There is no reason to force a bad fit.

### Qualification

This program is for current or incoming college athletes who want to take their personal brand and NIL activity seriously.

It is for athletes who are willing to create, communicate, follow through, and implement.

It is for athletes who want more professional systems, not magic tricks.

It is not for somebody who wants guaranteed deals, guaranteed income, or somebody else to build the entire thing while they stay passive.

It is not for somebody looking for a viral hack.

And it is not for somebody who thinks NIL is a lottery ticket.

### CTA

## Word-for-word script

Short answer: no, you do not need a huge following for NIL to matter.

But I also do not want to give you the fake answer that follower count never matters.

It can matter.

A larger audience can create more reach, and more reach can make certain types of partnerships more attractive.

The mistake is turning that into, "If I do not have a massive following, I have nothing to offer."

Those are two completely different statements.

A brand can look at more than your follower count.

They can care about who your audience is, how engaged that audience is, whether your identity fits the category, whether your content is useful, whether you have local relevance, whether people trust you, whether you communicate professionally, whether you can actually deliver what you promise, and whether the partnership makes sense for the people they want to reach.

Think about it this way.

Ten thousand random followers and ten thousand people who strongly identify with you are not automatically the same commercial asset.

And one hundred thousand followers with no clear identity does not automatically make every brand a fit.

So the question I want you asking is not just, "How do I get more followers?"

Ask, "How do I become easier for the right brand to understand and work with?"

That changes what you focus on.

You focus on positioning.

You focus on consistent content themes.

You focus on knowing what types of partnerships actually make sense for you.

You focus on professional assets.

You focus on building relationships.

You focus on being reliable when an opportunity actually shows up.

And yes, while you do all of that, you can still grow your audience.

The point is that audience growth becomes one part of a larger marketability system instead of the only thing you think matters.

So if you are going into the call thinking, "I only have 2,000 followers, so I probably do not belong here," do not disqualify yourself before we even look at the situation.

Bring the real numbers.

Bring the real profile.

Let us look at what you already have, what is unclear, and what can actually be improved.

That is a much more useful conversation than deciding your entire NIL future from one follower count.

- confirmation hero play

- breakout 04 play
- breakout 05 play

- total minutes consumed before the scheduled call
